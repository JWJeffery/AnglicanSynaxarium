Today the Church celebrates the Nativity of our Lord Jesus Christ, the eternal Son of the Father, who was born of the Virgin Mary for us and for our salvation.

In the days of the Emperor Augustus, a decree for registration brought Joseph from Nazareth in Galilee to Bethlehem in Judea. He belonged to the house of David, and Bethlehem was David's city. Mary went with him, carrying the child conceived in her by the Holy Spirit.

While they were there, the time came for her to give birth. Mary brought forth her firstborn son. She wrapped him in bands of cloth and laid him in a manger, a feeding place for animals, because there was no room for them in the lodging.

The Son through whom the world was made entered the world as a child who needed to be held, wrapped, and fed. Mary cared for the one who sustained her life and the life of every creature.

Nearby, shepherds kept watch over their flocks through the night. An angel of the Lord stood before them, and the glory of the Lord shone around them. They were terrified.

The angel told them not to fear. He brought good news of great joy for all the people. In David's city a Savior had been born, the Messiah, the Lord. They would find the child wrapped in cloth and lying in a manger.

Then a multitude of the heavenly host joined the angel, praising God and proclaiming peace on earth.

When the angels had gone, the shepherds set out for Bethlehem. They found Mary and Joseph, and the child lying in the manger. The sign was as the angel had said. They told what they had heard about him, and those who listened marveled. Mary treasured these things and pondered them in her heart.

The shepherds returned to their flocks, glorifying and praising God for what they had heard and seen.

The Church receives their testimony and worships the child they found. He is the Word who was with God in the beginning and who is God. All things came into being through him. In him is life, and that life is the light of humanity. This eternal Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us.

The Lord took our humanity into his own life. He received a human body and a human soul, a mother's care, a people's history, and the vulnerability of birth. The one Mary held was truly God and truly human, one Lord Jesus Christ. His coming opened our life to the life of God.

This is the joy of the Nativity. The Son came to make us children of his Father. He entered the life wounded by sin and death to heal it, bear it through his cross, and raise it in his resurrection. Those who receive him are given a birth that comes from God.

In Holy Baptism we are joined to Christ, and by the Holy Spirit we begin to live the new life he gives. At his table he nourishes us with his body and blood. Day by day, his grace renews the humanity he has made his own.

The feast calls us to receive this grace in the lives we actually lead. The Lord who accepted Mary's care meets us among people who need our care. His humility draws us from pride; his mercy frees us to forgive; his peace teaches us to lay down enmity.

With the shepherds, let us come to Christ and worship. With Mary, let us treasure what God has done. May the Son who shared our nature renew us in his likeness and bring us into the everlasting joy of his Father's house.

## Collect

Almighty God, you have given your only-begotten Son to take our nature upon him, and to be born of a pure virgin: Grant that we, who have been born again and made your children by adoption and grace, may daily be renewed by your Holy Spirit; through our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom with you and the same Spirit be honor and glory, now and for ever. Amen.
