Today the Church commemorates Kamehameha and Emma of Hawai‘i, who sought Christ in the Church's prayer and gave their care and resources to the sick and the children of their people.

Kamehameha IV was born Alexander Liholiho in 1834. He became King in 1855 and married Emma the following year. Emma had grown up in the household of her adoptive father, Doctor Thomas Rooke. Their country was an independent Hawaiian kingdom. Diseases brought from abroad were taking lives among its people.

The King and Queen set about raising money for a hospital. They went from house to house asking for gifts and contributed from their own resources. In 1859 the Queen's Hospital opened in Honolulu. People who could not afford treatment had a place to seek care.

That September the King shot and gravely wounded his secretary, Henry Neilson, after suspecting a relationship between Neilson and Emma. Neilson survived the shooting but never recovered his health. He died in 1862. The King later acknowledged that the act had been planned and that his suspicions had been wrong. He expressed remorse and proposed to submit to a trial and give up the throne. His ministers and the governing bodies opposed his abdication. No trial took place.

In 1860, the King and Queen sought to establish an Anglican presence in Hawai‘i. They approached Samuel Wilberforce, Bishop of Oxford, seeking a bishop and clergy. The Church they desired would bring the sacraments and the Prayer Book's common worship into the life of their kingdom.

In August 1862 their only child, Prince Albert, fell gravely ill. The King and Queen stayed beside him while doctors tried to help him. With the Bishop still on his journey, Ephraim Clark, a Congregationalist minister, baptized Albert using the Anglican service on August 23. The Prince died four days later. He was four years old.

Bishop Thomas Staley reached Honolulu in October. The grieving parents received him, and he began the work for which they had invited him. The King was already translating the Prayer Book into Hawaiian. Emma received baptism and gave the service's responses from memory. On November 28, 1862, the anniversary of the recognition of Hawaiian independence, Staley confirmed the King and Queen in the Anglican Church.

The King continued to work on the translation in his spare time. He wanted his people to take their own part in worship. He explained that the congregation needed words it could offer together, so that everyone could pray rather than merely listen to the Priest. He also wrote of the life Christians shared with their unseen Redeemer through receiving his Body in the Eucharist. The book placed that common prayer and sacramental life within the reach of Hawaiian speakers.

On a visit to a village church after his son's death, the King heard a sermon in the morning and offered to speak to the people later that day. In the afternoon he stood before the congregation and spoke on the words, "Jesus wept." He told them of God's love and the hope that love gave to people in sorrow. The tears of Christ at the grave of Lazarus belonged within the King's own grief.

His health failed. Emma cared for him through his last illness, and he died on November 30, 1863. She had lost her son and her husband within little more than a year.

Emma continued the work they had begun. In 1865 she traveled to Britain to seek support for the Church in Hawai‘i. She met Queen Victoria, who had also been widowed, and visited churches to raise funds. The cathedral she helped to build was named for Saint Andrew, whose feast fell on the day of the King's death.

She also sought teachers for Hawaiian girls. In 1867 she founded Saint Andrew's Priory School with the help of Anglican Sisters. The Sisters offered the girls an education that joined learning to Christian worship.

Emma remained active in the affairs of her country. In 1874 she became a candidate for the throne, but the Legislature elected Kalākaua. She continued to support the hospital, the schools, and the Church. Her care for her people endured through widowhood and disappointment.

She died in April 1885. Her royal funeral brought her body to the mausoleum where her husband and son lay.

Kamehameha and Emma used their royal wealth to care for the sick and helped their people to share in the Church's worship. Christ sustained that service through sorrow, and Emma continued it in widowhood. In their fellowship, we offer him the gifts entrusted to us for the care of his people.

## Collect

O God, who called your servants Kamehameha and Emma to an earthly throne that they might advance your heavenly kingdom, and gave them zeal for your church and love for your people: Mercifully grant that we also may be fruitful in good works, and attain to the glorious crown of your saints; through Jesus Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, one God, for ever and ever. Amen.
