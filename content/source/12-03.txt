**Francis Xavier, Priest and Missionary — December 3rd**

Today the Church commemorates Francis Xavier, priest and missionary, who carried the Gospel from Europe to India, Southeast Asia, and Japan, and who spent his last days waiting for a passage into China.

Francis was born in the kingdom of Navarre in 1506 and went to Paris as a young man to study. There he met Ignatius of Loyola. Their friendship changed the direction of his life. In 1534, Francis joined Ignatius and five other companions in making vows of poverty and chastity and promising to serve Christ. Their fellowship became the Society of Jesus. Francis was ordained a priest in 1537, and a few years later he accepted a mission that would carry him far beyond the world he knew.

He sailed under the patronage of the Portuguese crown and reached Goa in 1542. The port was both a center of Christian worship and a stronghold of Portugal's overseas empire. Francis visited the sick, taught children, ministered to prisoners, and sought to strengthen Christians who had received baptism but little instruction.

From Goa he traveled to the fishing villages near the southern tip of India. Along the coast he found Christians who could tell him that they had been baptized but who knew little of the prayers and teaching of the Church. Francis could not yet speak their language. In a letter to his Jesuit companions, he described how he gathered people able to translate with him. Together they prepared Christian instruction in the language used by the villagers.

He learned the translated catechism by heart, then went from village to village ringing a bell to gather children and adults. He taught the Lord's Prayer, the Creed, and the commandments. He urged the children to carry what they had learned back to their parents and neighbors. The work was tiring and often repetitive. It joined the celebration of the sacraments to the patient labor of helping a community learn to pray.

Francis traveled onward through the islands and ports of Southeast Asia. He lived among people whose customs he could not always understand and depended repeatedly on translators and local helpers. Distance, sickness, danger at sea, and the lack of clergy pressed hard upon his mission. New Christians were often abused or enslaved, and some were killed.

In 1549, Francis reached Japan, where he began learning the language and preparing a catechism. He later traveled north to Kyoto, the imperial capital, hoping to meet the emperor. Civil strife and local resistance made his missionary work difficult. Nevertheless, he came away with deep respect for the Japanese people and their culture.

China then drew his attention. Francis returned toward India and set out again in 1552, hoping to enter a country largely closed to foreigners. He waited on an island off the Chinese coast for a vessel that would take him to the mainland. The passage never came. He fell ill and died there on December 3, 1552, aged forty-six. His body was eventually carried back to Goa.

Francis died within sight of the country he hoped to enter. The letters he left behind preserve his longing that people should hear the Gospel, receive baptism, and grow in the life of the Church. His long journeys, his care for those he instructed, and his willingness to begin again among strangers have remained part of the Church's memory of him. With Francis, we ask God to raise up heralds of Christ's kingdom who will give themselves to the patient work of proclaiming the Gospel.

Let us pray.

God of all nations: Raise up in this and every land evangelists and heralds of your kingdom, that like your servant Francis Xavier we may proclaim the unsearchable riches of our Savior Jesus Christ; who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, one God, now and for ever. Amen.
